Comparing penguin body mass across species

Author

Cesar Figueroa

Published

September 24, 2026

Question

How does body mass vary across Adelie, Chinstrap, and Gentoo penguins??? Hmm let’s see.

Data source

Data: Palmer Penguins, collected by Dr. Kristen Gorman and Palmer Station Antarctica LTER. The data are available under a CC0 license.

Load the data

from palmerpenguins import load_penguins

Prepare the data

Two observations have missing body mass (Na). I exclude these two rows from the comparison and keep observations with missing values in unrelated columns.

penguins_clean = penguins.dropna(subset=["body_mass_g"]).copy()

print(f"Original rows: {len(penguins)}")
print(f"Rows retained: {len(penguins_clean)}")
print(f"Rows removed: {len(penguins) - len(penguins_clean)}")

Compare body mass across species

I calculate the number of observations, mean, and median body mass for each species. Body mass is measured in grams.

mass_summary = (
    penguins_clean.groupby("species")["body_mass_g"]
    .agg(["count", "mean", "median"])
    .round(1)
)

Findings :O

Gentoo penguins have the highest mean body mass in this sample: approximately 5,076 g, compared with 3,701 g for Adelie and 3,733 g for Chinstrap. The median follows the same pattern: 5,000 g for Gentoo and 3,700 g for both other species.

These are descriptive comparisons of the observed penguins. They do not establish that species alone explains the differences; this analysis does not account for factors such as sex, island, or year. Two observations with missing body mass were excluded.

Visualize body mass

The boxplot compares the median, spread, and potential outliers of body mass across species.

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

species = ["Adelie", "Chinstrap", "Gentoo"]
mass_by_species = [
    penguins_clean.loc[
        penguins_clean["species"] == name, "body_mass_g"
    ]
    for name in species
]

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(7, 4))
ax.boxplot(mass_by_species, tick_labels=species)
ax.set_xlabel("Species")
ax.set_ylabel("Body mass (g)")
fig.tight_layout()
plt.show()
